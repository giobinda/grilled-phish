How to spot a scam website

A 5-minute guide. No technical knowledge needed.

1. What is “phishing”?

Phishing (say “fishing”) is when criminals pretend to be someone you trust, such as your bank, the post office, Amazon, the tax office or a relative, to make you click a link and then type a password, a card number or a code on a fake website.

The fake website can look exactly like the real one: same logo, same colors, same pages. Copying a website now takes minutes. So the look of a page proves nothing. The only thing a criminal can't copy is the website's address.

2. Read the address, not the page

The address is shown at the top of the browser. The part that matters is the name just before the first single “/”, together with its ending (.com, .it…). Everything before it can be invented by anyone.

✅ Realhttps://www.paypal.com/signin
⛔ Fakehttps://paypal.com.secure-login.net/signin

This website is secure-login.net. “paypal.com” at the start is just decoration.

⛔ Fakehttps://www.paypal-account-help.com

Extra words such as “help”, “secure”, “login” or “verify” are added by scammers, not by PayPal.

3. Look-alike letters

Scammers swap letters that look the same, especially in small print or for tired eyes:

The last one is impossible to see, even with perfect eyesight. That is why Grilled Phish checks the letters for you and highlights the ones that are different.

4. Warning signs in messages

Most scams start with an email, a text message, a WhatsApp message or a phone call. Be suspicious when it:

  • ⏰ Rushes you: “within 24 hours”, “your account will be closed”, “last warning”.
  • 😨 Scares you: a blocked card, a fine, a suspicious payment, a problem with the police.
  • 🎁 Promises something: a prize, a refund, a package waiting for a small fee.
  • 🔑 Asks for a password, a card number or a code you received by text message.
  • 👋 Comes from a “new number”: “Hi Mum, I lost my phone, can you pay this bill for me?”
  • 🔗 Contains a link to “check”, “confirm”, “unlock” or “update” something.

5. Five rules that stop almost every scam

  1. Don't use links in messages to log in. Open your bank's app, or type the address yourself, or use a bookmark you saved earlier.
  2. Never tell anyone a code you received by text message. Not the bank, not the police, not “technical support”. Real companies never ask for it.
  3. Hang up and call back. If someone calls about your money, end the call and call the number printed on your card or on the official website.
  4. Take your time. Real banks and real offices can wait an hour. Only scammers are in a hurry.
  5. Ask someone you trust before paying or typing a password when something feels strange. Scammers often say “don't tell anyone”: that alone is a warning sign.

7. Did you already click or type something?

Don't be ashamed: it happens to careful, intelligent people every day. Act quickly:

  1. If you gave card or bank details: call your bank now, using the number on your card, and ask them to block the card.
  2. If you typed a password: change it on the real website, and on every other website where you used the same one.
  3. If you installed a program or let someone “connect” to your computer: turn off the internet and ask someone you trust or a technician to check it.
  4. Report it (see below). It helps get the fake website closed for everyone.
